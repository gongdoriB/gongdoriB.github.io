Research

Across projects I ask one question: when the input a person gives a system changes — a head movement, the language of a query, a piece of feedback — how does the system's response change, and where exactly does it fail? Preliminary results are marked as such.

Query–Interface Language Mismatch in Multilingual GUI Reasoning

CUAI Advanced Track · two-person team, first author · Jul 2026 – present Grand Prize

How badly does a vision-language model misread a GUI when the user's question and the on-screen text are in different languages, and where does the loss come from?

  • Initial study (summer 2026). A fully crossed 6×6 query–GUI language design over 2,195 MPR-GUI-Bench items with two VLMs, analysed with item-level GLMMs. The mismatch penalty concentrated in text-dependent relational reasoning (about 12%p) and was near zero for text-independent items. A two-stage localization method recovered part of it (+4.22%p, +8.80%p).
  • Follow-up (Sep 2026 – present, preliminary). Query variants that change referent names and sentence language separately, across six languages and seven VLMs. Replacing only the referent names with on-screen text improved accuracy for all seven models (+4.7 to +12.0%p); changing only the sentence language slightly lowered it. Manuscript in preparation.

Answer Correction in Language Models Following User Feedback

INC LAB · first author · Oct – Nov 2026 · submitted to DigiCon-26

Instructions that stop a model from caving to a wrong user claim (sycophancy) — do they also stop it from accepting a correct one?

  • Split an anti-sycophancy instruction into its clauses and varied the rationale attached to the user's correction; Qwen3-8B on MMLU-Pro with paired item-bootstrap CIs.
  • The inhibition clause cut authority-backed wrong agreement from 77.4% to 40.4%, but also cut valid correction from 81.9% to 53.9%. Attaching a same-item rationale reduced this cost from 28.57 to 6.49 points.

HMD-Based Head-Gesture Recognition for Hands-Free XR Interaction

INC LAB · first author, with teammates · 2026 Gold Prize

Can the sensors already inside a headset provide a hands-free input channel for XR?

  • Collected 1,440 three-second samples of six deliberate head gestures from eight participants with Meta Quest 3 orientation and angular-velocity signals (Unity, OpenXR).
  • Compared Random Forest, CNN1D and LSTM under leave-one-trial-out and leave-one-subject-out evaluation. CNN1D reached the highest Macro F1 in both (0.958 LOTO, 0.970 LOSO; mean of three seeds).
