Research

My research focuses on language and multimodal models, particularly in the context of human-AI interaction: how models understand user inputs and respond when people check and correct them. Most of my recent work lies here. I have also worked on XR interaction, where systems take input from how people move and let them explore real-world data. Across both, I look at what happens when the input a person gives a system changes, and where exactly the system fails.

Language & Multimodal Models

Query–Interface Language Mismatch in Multilingual GUI Reasoning

CUAI Advanced Track · two-person team, first author · Jul 2026 – present Grand Prize

How badly does a vision-language model misread a GUI when the user's question and the on-screen text are in different languages, and where does the loss come from?

  • Initial study (summer 2026). A fully crossed 6×6 query–GUI language design over 2,195 MPR-GUI-Bench items with Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL2.5-8B, analysed with item-level GLMMs. The mismatch penalty concentrated in text-dependent relational reasoning (about 12%p) and was near zero for text-independent items. A two-stage localization method recovered part of it (+4.22%p, +8.80%p).
  • Follow-up (Sep 2026 – present). Extending the study to six languages and seven VLMs, separating the effects of on-screen referent names from sentence language, and comparing machine-translated queries with queries that use on-screen names. Ongoing and not yet published.

Answer Correction in Language Models Following User Feedback

INC LAB · first author · Oct – Nov 2026 · accepted to DigiCon-26 (to appear) · recommended for the Best Paper Award by both reviewers

Instructions that stop a model from caving to a wrong user claim (sycophancy) — do they also stop it from accepting a correct one?

  • Split an anti-sycophancy instruction into its clauses and varied the rationale attached to the user's correction; Qwen3-8B on MMLU-Pro with paired item-bootstrap CIs.
  • The inhibition clause cut authority-backed wrong agreement from 77.4% to 40.4%, but also cut valid correction from 81.9% to 53.9%. Attaching a same-item rationale reduced this cost from 28.6 to 6.5 percentage points.

HMD-Based Head-Gesture Recognition for Hands-Free XR Interaction

INC LAB · first author, with teammates · KDCS 2026 paper Gold Prize · IWCS 2026 poster

Can the sensors already inside a headset provide a hands-free input channel for XR?

  • Collected 1,440 three-second samples of six deliberate head gestures from eight participants with Meta Quest 3 orientation and angular-velocity signals (Unity, OpenXR).
  • Compared Random Forest, CNN1D and LSTM under leave-one-trial-out and leave-one-subject-out evaluation. CNN1D reached the highest Macro F1 in both (0.958 LOTO, 0.970 LOSO; mean of three seeds; IWCS poster results).
